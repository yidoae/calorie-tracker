# Starter guidelines (summary)
Source: Starter summary written for FitBot from the guidelines cited in each section. Replace or extend it with the original documents.

## Protein needs for exercising people
Based on: Jäger R, et al. ISSN position stand: protein and exercise. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2017;14:20.

For most exercising individuals, a daily protein intake of about 1.4–2.0 g per kg of body weight is sufficient to build and maintain muscle mass. Higher intakes (around 2.3–3.1 g per kg of lean body mass) may help preserve lean mass during a calorie deficit in resistance-trained people. Spreading protein evenly over the day, roughly every 3–4 hours, with about 0.25 g per kg of body weight (or 20–40 g) per meal, is a practical approach.

## Creatine monohydrate
Based on: Kreider RB, et al. ISSN position stand: safety and efficacy of creatine supplementation in exercise, sport, and medicine. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2017;14:18.

Creatine monohydrate is the most studied and effective form of creatine for increasing high-intensity exercise capacity and lean mass during training. A common protocol is a loading phase of about 0.3 g per kg of body weight per day (roughly 20 g/day split into four doses) for 5–7 days, followed by a maintenance dose of 3–5 g per day. Taking 3–5 g per day without loading also saturates muscle stores, just more slowly (about 3–4 weeks).

## Physical activity for adults
Based on: WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour. World Health Organization, 2020.

Adults aged 18–64 should do at least 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week, or 75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity, or an equivalent combination. They should also do muscle-strengthening activities involving all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week. Doing more than 300 minutes of moderate activity brings additional health benefits, and any activity is better than none; sedentary time should be limited.

## Sleep for adults
Based on: Watson NF, et al. Recommended amount of sleep for a healthy adult: a joint consensus statement of the AASM and SRS. Sleep. 2015;38(6):843–844.

Adults should sleep 7 or more hours per night on a regular basis to promote optimal health. Regularly sleeping less than 7 hours is associated with adverse health outcomes, including weight gain, impaired performance and poorer recovery.

## Energy content of macronutrients
Based on: Atwater general factors, as used in food labelling.

Protein provides about 4 kcal per gram, carbohydrate about 4 kcal per gram, fat about 9 kcal per gram and alcohol about 7 kcal per gram. A deficit of roughly 7,700 kcal corresponds to about 1 kg of body fat, which is why a daily deficit of about 500 kcal leads to roughly 0.5 kg of weight loss per week, although real-world progress is rarely linear.
